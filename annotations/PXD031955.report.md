## Study

Technology-benchmark study: capillary-electrophoresis ESI-HRMS demonstrated to scale across two
vertebrate models of very different size — giant (~250 µm) blastomeres of a 16-cell *Xenopus laevis*
embryo, and small (~35 µm) cultured mouse hippocampal neurons.

| | |
|---|---|
| Publication | PMID:35464213 / PMC9024316 / doi:10.3389/fchem.2022.863979 (open access, Methods read in full) |
| Instruments | **Q Exactive Plus** (CE-ESI, 15 runs) and **Orbitrap Fusion Lumos** (nanoLC, 3 runs) |
| Acquisition | DDA, label-free throughout |
| Isolation | capillary microsampling / microaspiration |
| Screen row | `include` — label free, carrier=no, `~3 neurons` |

**PRIDE lists no publication for PXD031955** (`publications: []`). The link is nonetheless certain:
the paper's own *Data Repository* section names `PXD031955` explicitly, and the sibling deposition
PXD030607 — identical title, description and protocols — does link PMID:35464213.

## The screen row was right to flag this, and the brief's warning was correct

The screen said `organism=Xenopus laevis` with cell type "blastomere and hippocampal neuron". This
**is** a mixed-organism deposition. PRIDE's `organism` field lists both *Mus musculus* and
*Xenopus laevis*. The hippocampal neurons are **mouse**, not Xenopus:

> "Primary cultures of isolated mouse hippocampal neurons were prepared as described elsewhere"

9 rows are Xenopus, 9 are mouse. `vertebrates` covers both (amphibian + mammal), so one organism
template suffices; `human` would have been wrong on every row.

## ⚠️ PXD031955 is a strict superset of PXD030607 — 15 runs are byte-identical duplicates

This is the single most important finding for the caller, and it is not a guess — it is checksum
evidence from `files/all` on both accessions:

| | PXD031955 | PXD030607 |
|---|---|---|
| RAW files | **18** | 15 |

All 15 of PXD030607's raw files appear in PXD031955 with **identical checksums**. PXD031955 adds
exactly 3 files, which exist in no other deposition:

- `2017-11-10_SC02_Single_Neuron_microtube_injection_5_min.RAW`
- `2017-11-17_SC01._Single_Neuron_microvial_injection_5_min.RAW`
- `2017-11-17_SC04_ziptip_Single_Neuron_microvial_injection_5_min.RAW`

Those three are the study's **actual single neurons** (Table S5). PXD030607 contains no single-neuron
data at all — despite the screen row for this publication citing "~3 neurons".

**Consequence:** `annotations/PXD031955.sdrf.tsv` and `annotations/PXD030607.sdrf.tsv` describe the
same 15 runs twice. PXD031955 is the canonical, complete deposition (it is the accession the paper
cites, and it is a superset). PXD030607 appears to be an earlier/partial submission of the same study.
**Recommendation: contribute PXD031955 and drop PXD030607, or the community repo will carry duplicate
annotations of 15 identical raw files.** This is a caller decision, not mine — I did not touch
PXD030607's files.

I annotated PXD031955 independently before reading the sibling artifact. Our conclusions converge on
essentially every shared value (instrument split, cleavage stage, blastoderm cell, modification
split, collision energies, scan ranges), which is reassuring but is **not** independent verification —
same paper, same spec. Two deviations are noted under *Judgment calls* below.

## Key decisions and evidence

**Instrument — the artifact contradicts PRIDE, confirmed against the raw bytes.** PRIDE's
`instruments` field says `Orbitrap Fusion Lumos` and **`Q Exactive`**. `Q Exactive` is wrong: the paper
says "Q Exactive **Plus**", and HTTP range-reading the first 256 KB of **all 18** deposited raw files
and decoding utf-16-le returns `Q Exactive Plus` in 15 and `Orbitrap Fusion Lumos` in exactly the 3
`LP_AWCsinglecellV12_*.raw` nanoLC runs. Annotated `MS:1002634` / `MS:1002732`. Paper and raw bytes
agree against PRIDE; the header scan also independently confirms which runs are the nanoLC arm.

**Modifications differ per arm — PRIDE and the paper conflict, and the paper wins on chemistry.**
PRIDE's `data_processing_protocol` says: "Static and dynamic modifications were **disabled for Xenopus
data** and enabled for analyzing mouse neuronal data." The paper says: "The static and dynamic
modifications of cysteine carbamidomethylation and methionine oxidation were enabled for
neuroproteomics by CE-MS **and Xenopus proteomics by LC-MS**."

The paper is right, and the sample prep proves it independently:

- Xenopus **CE** — "traditional bottom-up proteomics steps such as reduction, alkylation, and desalting
  were **eliminated** to reduce material losses". No alkylation ⇒ Carbamidomethyl cannot be searched
  ⇒ `comment[modification parameters] = not applicable` (both columns), and reduction/alkylation
  reagent = `not applicable`. **6 rows.**
- Xenopus **nanoLC** — "the proteome was similarly processed, **including reduction and alkylation**,
  and desalted on a ZipTip" ⇒ Carbamidomethyl fixed + Oxidation variable. **3 rows.**
- Mouse (all 9) — "enabled for neuroproteomics by CE-MS" ⇒ Carbamidomethyl fixed + Oxidation variable.

PRIDE's blanket "disabled for Xenopus data" would have been wrong on the 3 nanoLC rows. `UNIMOD:4` /
`UNIMOD:35` verified; no `UNIMOD:1`/`UNIMOD:21` involved.

**Per-arm technical values** (all from Methods, none inherited across arms):

| | Xenopus CE | Xenopus nanoLC | Mouse CE |
|---|---|---|---|
| collision energy | 28 NCE | 30 NCE | 32 NCE |
| fragment tolerance | 0.02 Da | **0.6 Da** | 0.02 Da |
| MS2 analyzer | orbitrap | **radial ejection linear ion trap** | orbitrap |
| MS1 scan range | 350–900 m/z | not available | 350–1800 m/z |

Precursor tolerance is 10 ppm everywhere. The 0.6 Da / ion-trap pairing for the Lumos is stated twice
over: "fragment mass tolerance, 0.02 Da (Q Exactive Plus) and **0.6 Da (Lumos)**" and "The precursor
ions were fragmented in the **ion trap**". Note the Methods are internally inconsistent here — they say
"fragmented in the ion trap" while also specifying HCD, which occurs in the HCD cell. The 0.6 Da
tolerance independently corroborates low-resolution **ion-trap MS2 detection**, so `ion trap` is
annotated as the MS2 analyzer and HCD (`MS:1000422`) as the dissociation method. Lumos MS1 scan range
is genuinely not stated → `not available`.

**"Dopaminergic neurons" in PRIDE is wrong — ignored.** PRIDE's `sample_processing_protocol` says
"Microsampling was also used to access the soma legions of **dopaminergic** neurons." The paper's
abstract, Methods, and Table S5 all say **hippocampal**, and the mouse tissue is the hippocampus. The
PRIDE sentence (which also contains "legions" for "regions" and a duplicated "electrospray ionization")
is submitter boilerplate carried over from another submission. Annotated `hippocampal neuron`
(CL:0002608) / `hippocampal formation` (UBERON:0002421).

**`hippocampus` triggered exactly the OLS failure the brief warns about.** `searchClasses(query=
"hippocampus", ontologyId="uberon")` in smart mode returned a **single confident hit**:
`CA1 field of hippocampus` (UBERON:0003881) — a sub-region, wrong, and returned with no `fallback`
flag. Fuzzy mode exposed 56 candidates. Resolved to `hippocampal formation` (UBERON:0002421) by an
exact query. Every accession in the artifact was verified live against OLS.

## Judgment calls (the two that most deserve reviewer scrutiny)

**1. `single cell isolation protocol` — the enum does not contain this study's method.** The fixed enum
is FACS / cellenONE / microfluidics / laser capture microdissection / manual picking / nanoPOTS /
droplet microfluidics / acoustic droplet ejection. The study uses **capillary microsampling**, which is
absent. The validator's `error_level` is `warning`, so a free-text value was possible. I split the
column on a physical distinction rather than using one value throughout:

- **Mouse single neurons → `manual picking` (3 rows).** A whole cell really is isolated and removed:
  "Capillary microsampling enabled the **isolation and transfer of individual neurons** from the
  culture"; Table S5 says the neurons "were microaspirated **from** culture". This is manual,
  micromanipulator-based, and is neither FACS, microfluidic, LCM, nor nanoPOTS — `manual picking` is
  the closest true enum member (same reasoning as the accepted `PXD023366` annotation).
- **Xenopus blastomeres → `not applicable` (6 CE + 3 nanoLC rows).** **No cell is ever isolated.** The
  blastomere stays in the intact embryo and only its *contents* are aspirated in situ. "Isolation
  protocol" has no referent, and `allow_not_applicable = true`.

The sibling PXD030607 annotation uses `not applicable` on all 15 of its rows. We agree on the 15 shared
rows; the 3 single-neuron rows are unique to this deposition and are, in my reading, genuine cell
isolation. A reviewer who prefers `capillary microsampling` as free text (accepting the warning, and
more faithful to the paper's own terminology) has a reasonable case — I chose enum membership because
the value is machine-filtered.

**2. Mouse `developmental stage` = `not available` — a deliberate abstention.** The column is
`required` but `allow_not_available = true`. The temptation was `embryo stage`: the paper says
"**pregnant** C57BL/6 dams" and cites Kaech & Banker (2006), a canonical **E18** hippocampal-culture
protocol. I rejected the inference. "Pregnant dams" is equally consistent with harvesting **P0 pups**
— a routine practice, and the reason labs buy pregnant dams at all — and the cited protocol is a *rat*
E18 method whose stage cannot be transferred to a mouse adaptation without a second inference hop. The
paper never states an embryonic day, or even the word "embryo", for the mouse. Two chained inferences
to fill an optional-in-practice slot is not worth the fabrication risk.

Xenopus developmental stage **is** stated ("cultured to the **16-cell stage**") → `cleavage stage`
(UBERON:0000107, resolvable within EFO, which the column requires).

## Partial-cell aspiration — what the Methods actually support

The brief flagged this correctly. Capillary microsampling here **does** take only part of the cell, and
the paper quantifies it: 18 ng is "**~0.2% of the total cellular proteome**". A V11/V12 blastomere thus
holds ~9 µg, so even the 400 ng nanoLC aliquot is ~4% of one cell. The Xenopus rows are **subcellular
aspirates from an identified single cell**, not whole cells.

`characteristics[cells per well] = 1` is nonetheless correct and is not an over-claim: the column is
defined as "Number of cells per well/reaction", its pattern is `^\d+$` (no fractional value is
expressible), and the material in each reaction derives from **exactly one** identified cell. The
fraction sampled is recorded nowhere in the SDRF — see the gap below. `sample type = single cell`
(PRIDE:0000897) carries the same meaning: material representing one cell.

The mouse single neurons are different — those are whole cells, aspirated and transferred entire.

## Spec defect found: `characteristics[mass]` is unlicensed

The one thing this dataset most needs to record — **the amount of proteome analyzed per run**
(18 ng / 5 ng / 400 ng / 500 pg / 250 pg / 125 pg), which is the study's actual independent variable —
has no licensed column.

`TERMS.tsv` defines `mass` with `usage: sample-metadata` and the description "**Injected mass of the
sample.** Used for spiked-in experiments and quantitative comparisons" — a perfect fit. But
`characteristics[mass]` is **defined in no template YAML at all** (`grep` across
`spec/sdrf-proteomics/sdrf-templates/` returns nothing; `sample-metadata.yaml` defines
`characteristics[tissue mass]` but not `characteristics[mass]`), and it is absent from the 80-column
resolved merge. Per CLAUDE.md the resolved YAML is authoritative, so the column is **not licensed** and
I did not use it. This is genuine TERMS.tsv↔template drift and is worth an upstream issue.

**Mitigation:** the amount is encoded in `source name` (`Xla_E6_V11_CE_18ng`, `Xla_V12_LC_400ng`,
`Mmu_hip_neuron_culture_500pg`, …) and is recoverable from `comment[data file]`. This is why the three
bulk dilution levels get distinct source names despite sharing one biological source — it is the only
place the dilution survives.

## Deliberate ambiguity preserved: the nanoLC V12 cell

Table S4 is titled "Comparison of proteins identified from **single V12 blastomere** using nanoLC
(400 ng) and CE (18 ng)". Whether the nanoLC arm and the CE arm sampled the **same physical V12 cell**
is never stated. The dates are suggestive (`2021_4-12` CE vs `2021-04-13` nanoLC — consecutive days),
and a single V12 cell easily holds both aliquots, but suggestion is not evidence.

I therefore kept `Xla_E6_V12_CE_18ng` and `Xla_V12_LC_400ng` as **separate source names** with separate
cell identifiers (`Xla_E6_V12`, `Xla_V12_LC`). The nanoLC filenames carry no embryo token, so `E6`
cannot be asserted for them. Merging would assert an identity the paper does not support; splitting
merely under-links. Splitting is the recoverable error.

## Factor value

`factor value[cell type]` — blastoderm cell (9) vs hippocampal neuron (9).

This is a **technology-benchmark dataset with no biological factor**: nothing is treated, diseased, or
compared between conditions. The real manipulated variables are the loaded amount and the separation
platform (CE vs nanoLC), and **neither has an SDRF column** (see the `characteristics[mass]` gap).
`cell type` is the closest honest proxy for the paper's stated axis — scalability "across broad
cellular dimensions", 250 µm blastomere vs 35 µm neuron — and it mirrors an existing characteristics
column, as a factor value must.

## Columns deliberately omitted

`characteristics[cell diameter]` — the paper gives "~250 µm" (Xenopus) and "~35 µm" (neurons), which is
tempting. Omitted: the column is defined as "Physical diameter of the isolated cell **if measured**",
and these are class-level approximations, not per-cell measurements. Writing 250 µm on the D11 rows
would be worse than an approximation — the abstract implies D11 is *smaller* ("supporting sensitivity
for smaller cells") without ever giving its diameter.

`comment[acquisition date]` — filenames carry dates in three inconsistent formats (`2021_4-8`,
`2021-06-24`, `2016-04-11`), and a filename date is not necessarily the acquisition date. Omitted
rather than inferred; the true dates are recoverable from the raw headers if wanted.

## Notes for the reviewer

- Sample handling varies across the 3 single neurons — SC02 microtube injection, SC01 microvial, SC04
  microvial **+ ZipTip desalting**. Recorded implicitly via `comment[data file]`; no licensed column
  expresses it. It does not affect any annotated value but is a real n=3 heterogeneity.
- The 6 bulk rows are treated as **one** pooled digest measured at 3 dilution levels × 2 technical
  replicates (`pooled sample = pooled`, `biological replicate = 1`). The Methods describe a single bulk
  preparation in the singular, and Table S6 reports one merged result per dilution level. But the runs
  span 2016-04-01 → 2016-04-11, and the paper never states whether one or several culture preparations
  were used. **This is an inference**; if those are separate preps, "technical replicate" is the wrong
  frame for the within-level pairs.
- The 2016 bulk runs and the 2017 single-neuron runs come from cultures a year apart — certainly
  different biological material. They are correctly not linked by any shared source name.
- `SC##` tokens are **per-day run counters, not cell IDs**: `SC02` and `SC04` appear in both the 2016
  bulk set and the 2017 single-neuron set. Cell identifiers were namespaced (`Mmu_neuron_SC01`) to
  prevent a false join.
- Several `.msf` search files reference runs that were never deposited as RAW (e.g.
  `2016-04-06_SC02_hip_cultured_neu_lower_ce_hv_20kv.msf`). Not a coverage gap — the SDRF describes
  deposited runs, and all 18 are annotated.
