### BRAF-WT vs BRAF-Kinase (S7/S8 vs S9/S10): the real weak link, with an unrecorded source conflict

Supp Fig S3B/S3D describe the authors' "BRAF-Kinase" result as:

- 8 up-regulated sites, 37.5 % shared with other constructs (Venn);
- no MAPK1/MAPK3 activation-loop rows (the kinase column is black there);
- one kinase-only row, **RAF1 S465**.

In the deposited data that profile matches **S7/S8**, which the producer labels BRAF‑WT:

- RAF1 S465 is +2.3/+2.6 in S7/S8 and is not quantified at all in S9/S10.
- S7/S8 has 7 up-sites, 3 of them shared (≈ 8 / 37.5 %).
- S7/S8 shows only weak MAPK1 Y175 (+0.6/+0.7).

S9/S10 does not match it:

- 14 up-sites, 10 of them shared (71 %);
- MAPK1 Y175 +1.7/+3.1 and MAPK3 Y165 +1.7/+3.7.

Two other author sources fit the producer's assignment instead:

- Supp Fig S6C: BRAF‑Kinase raises pERK by 4 h, BRAF‑WT only weakly.
- Supp Fig S3C KSEA: MAP2K1/2 and RAF1 are top hits for BRAF‑Kinase. For BRAF‑WT the top hits are RPS6KB1, PRKCA, ATM, PRKCG and AKT2, with no MAPK kinases.

The construct sequence (N‑terminal BRAF transgene sites in S7/S8, none in S9/S10) is decisive about which pair carries the truncated construct. I therefore think the producer's assignment is the better-supported one. Campaign rule 6, however, requires that a conflict between sources be **recorded**.

The manifest says something different. It claims the S3D rank order "matches" (Kinase 8 ↔ S9/S10 14). It names WT/V600E as the weakest link. It does not mention that the authors' labelled "BRAF‑Kinase" phenotype (RAF1 S465, no MAPK, 8 sites) is the S7/S8 profile.

## Findings

### F1 — important (provenance / design): WT↔Kinase conflict not recorded; the weakest link is misstated

- Location: `characteristics[genetic modification]` and `factor value[genetic modification]`, rows of S7–S10 (24 rows). Also the manifest claim "characteristics[genetic modification]…" and limitations[1].
- Evidence: see above. RAF1 S465 is the only Fig S3B kinase-specific row, and it is quantified and induced only in S7/S8. The Fig S3D kinase set (8 sites, 37.5 % shared) matches S7/S8 (7 sites, 3 shared), not S9/S10 (14 sites, 10 shared). Fig S3B shows no MAPK rows in the kinase column, while S9/S10 has strong MAPK1/3 activation-loop induction. Against this, the N-terminal BRAF sites S151/S363/S365 are dox-induced in S7/S8 and absent from the aa 381+ kinase construct (construct primer), and Fig S6C and Fig S3C support the producer.
- Fix:
  1. Record the conflict in the manifest claim and limitations and in BLOCKED.md / report. Name the sources on each side, state that the transgene-sequence evidence was given priority under rule 6 ("data beat inference"), and add the RAF1 S465 / Venn numbers.
  2. Rewrite limitations[1] so that the weakest link is WT vs Kinase (S7/S8 vs S9/S10), not WT vs V600E.
  3. Add the CDC27 S364 / MARCKS S101 and fusion-marker-site cross-checks to the V600E and fusion evidence.
  4. Caller or user decides: keep S7–S10 with the conflict documented, or block S7–S10 (12 RAW) until an author key is available. If kept, the BLOCKED.md "unblocked by" text should ask for author confirmation of S7–S10 as well.

### F2 — minor (design/QC): S9/S10 label-specific artefact

- S9 and S10 have a large set of sites that do **not** flip with the label swap: 152 sites with the same raw H/L sign beyond ±1.5 (116 negative, 36 positive). Every other pair has ≤15.
- Their per-experiment SD is also wider: 0.88 / 0.92 vs 0.37–0.67 elsewhere.
- This points to a culture or labelling difference between the light and heavy cultures beyond dox. It does not change the annotation; note it in the limitations.

### F3 — minor (provenance): MAPK1 numbering

- The manifest cites "MAPK1 Y175". That is the numbering of the MaxQuant leading protein B4DHN0 (as in Fig S3B), not canonical P28482 Y187.
- Say so, so the claim can be checked.
