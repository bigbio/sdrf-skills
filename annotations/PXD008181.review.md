## What I tested
- **Files and .baf choice:** the PRIDE v3 list has 84 entries, and every entry's `projectAccessions` is PXD008181. They are 14 RAW, 14 .dat, 14 mzid, 14 mgf and 28 PRIDE-generated files. The RAW files are the renamed Bruker `analysis.baf` files, so citing `.baf` (not `.d`) is correct. All 14 are used, each on one heavy row and one light row. I verified the chain from .baf to mgf header path, `.dat FILE=` and mzid. No BroLi run is deposited.
- **Channel map:** the paper's Methods and Fig. 1 legend state that MCC cells were heavy (13C6-Lys) and HaCaT cells light, with arginine light in both. The Mascot search fixed Label:13C(6) on K. Passing K-containing chromogranin A PSMs (ELQDLALQGAK) appear in MKL_2_1 and Waga_122, which means heavy CHGA is present in the MCC channel. HaCaT keratinocytes do not express CHGA, so the data agree with the map.
- **Tolerances and mods from .dat:** I range-read the parameter headers of all 14 .dat files. All are identical: TOL 50 ppm, ITOL 0.5 Da, CLE Trypsin, PFA 2, MODS Carbamidomethyl (C) and Label:13C(6) (K) fixed, IT_MODS Acetyl (K) and Oxidation (M), ESI-QUAD-TOF. The SDRF matches. The label is written as MT=Variable (rule 11) and UNIMOD:188 is correct for +6.02 on K. The manifest records that Mascot searched with the label fixed (heavy only). This follows the campaign convention and is not a finding.
- **HaCaT reference encoding:** the rows use sample type `reference`, factor value `HaCaT`, biological replicate 1, `pooled`, and a separate source name for each mixture. The precedents PXD005854 and PXD009766 use factor `not applicable`, but there the reference was a normalisation spike-in. Here HaCaT is the paper's actual comparator: Tables 1-2 report H/L ratios of MCC against HaCaT, and the cell-line factor clearly applies to it. A HaCaT factor level is therefore correct. Two small issues remain, listed as minor findings M1 and M2.
- **Biological replicate 1 + pooled:** the paper says "Three biological replicates were cultured per each cell line, and these were merged into one sample upon cell lysis". So each line is one sample, numbered 1 within its group. This does not conflict with rule 3.
- **Technical replicates in 2015 and 2016:** the mgf paths show run 1 in `150531silac` and runs 2-3 in `160817`; both MCC13 runs are in 150531. The .baf headers confirm two batches (otofControl 3.4.14 vs 3.4.16). They also show a method difference that the manifest does not mention (M3). The paper says "technical triplicates", and the 20 µl digest aliquots were stored frozen, so numbering these injections as technical replicates 1-3 is acceptable.
- **Ontology / Cellosaurus:** every term matches OLS exactly. Cellosaurus age, sex, site and population agree row by row: MKL-1 26Y M axillary LN Caucasian; MKL-2 72Y M with no site; PeTa 65Y M skin; WaGa 67Y M ascites; MCC13 80Y F cervical LN Caucasian; HaCaT 62Y M skin of back, keratinocyte, Caucasian. Disease is MONDO:0019210 for the MCC lines and `normal` for HaCaT.

## Findings
- **M1 (minor, design):** the HaCaT factor level departs from the reference precedents. Add the reason to the manifest: HaCaT is the comparator, not a spike-in.
- **M2 (minor, design):** HaCaT has five per-mix source names, so convert-openms gives it 5 MSstats BioReplicates (2, 4, 6, 8, 10), although the paper describes one merged lysate. Fix: use one HaCaT source name (rows stay unique through assay name), or state in the manifest that the per-mix names are not independent cultures. The manifest's current reason, "row coordinate stays unique", is not needed for uniqueness.
- **M3 (minor, provenance):** the .baf headers show that MKL1_RA1_01_8251 and MKL2_RA1_01_8254 used `3s_bruker_290min_silica.m` (cycle-time DDA). The other 12 runs used `20_precursor_290min_silica[.m|_nb.m]` (top-20, as in the paper), with otofControl 3.4.14 in 2015 and 3.4.16 in 2016. Add this to the limitations; a batch/method comment column is optional.
- **M4 (minor, ontology):** the fragmentation on a Q-TOF could be given more specifically as beam-type CID (MS:1000422). The generic CID term is not wrong.
