## Study
"Tunneling nanotube-mediated intercellular vesicle and protein transfer in the stroma-provided imatinib resistance in chronic myeloid leukemia cells". Kolba MD *et al.*, *Cell Death Dis* 2019;10(11):817, doi:10.1038/s41419-019-2045-8, PMID 31659149, PMC6817823.

The study used **trans-SILAC**:
- HS-5 bone-marrow stromal cells (the donors) were labelled with Lys 13C6 15N2 and Arg 13C6 15N4 for 9 days, then stained with the vesicle dye DiD.
- They were co-cultured for 24 h with GFP-expressing K-562 CML cells (the acceptors).
- The acceptor cells were FACS-sorted into DiD+ (received stromal vesicles) and DiD− populations.
- Each population was run on SDS-PAGE, cut into 6 slices and measured on a Q Exactive Plus.
- The experiment was done in two biological replicates.
- Heavy peptides inside the K-562 cells are stroma-derived proteins.

### Sources for the channel map (rule 6)
- **Label → origin.** The paper's Methods give it explicitly: "Donor cells were labeled with heavy isotopologues of lysine and arginine". The acceptor K-562 cells were unlabelled. So heavy = HS-5-derived protein and light = the K-562 proteome.
- **Experiment → population.** `samplenames.xls` and `mqpar.xml` assign `sample_1-6` = plus_01, `7-12` = minus_01, `13-18` = plus_02 and `19-24` = minus_02, with fractions 1-6. The plus/minus names match the paper's DiD+/DiD−.
- **Data check** (`proteinGroups.txt`; scripts `hl.py`, `heavy_sets.py`):
  - Light is 98.4-99.5% of the summed L+H intensity in every experiment, so the light channel is the acceptor proteome.
  - plus_ carries more heavy material than minus_: H/(L+H) is 1.62% / 1.20% for plus_ against 0.55% / 0.57% for minus_.
  - More heavy-detected protein groups occur in both replicates of plus_ (969) than of minus_ (808), at ≥2 unique peptides.
  - This matches the paper's finding of more donor-derived proteins in DiD+ (646) than in DiD− (481). The absolute numbers differ: the paper reports MaxQuant 1.5.3.12, while the deposited search is 1.6.0.1.
  - No conflict between sources.

### Trans-SILAC encoding (a modelling choice; review it)
- The light and heavy rows of a run are two channels of **one** sorted-K-562 lysate.
- They carry distinct source names because they describe protein of different cellular origin, and because the row coordinate (source, replicate, fraction) must be unique.
- Heavy rows carry the HS-5 cell-line metadata (CVCL_3720, bone-marrow stroma, normal, male 30Y), because the label marks the donor of the protein.
- Every row also states the physical sample in three custom columns:
  - `characteristics[co-culture]`
  - `characteristics[sorted cell population]`
  - `characteristics[protein origin]`
- No curated trans-SILAC precedent was found in `sdrf-annotated-datasets`.

### Biological replicates (rule 3)
- The paper says "trans-SILAC has been done in two biological replicates". These are experiments _01 and _02.
- They are numbered 1 and 2 within each sorted-population group.
- The light and heavy rows of one lysate share the number.

## Technical values
- **Instrument and acquisition** (raw headers of sample_1/7/13/19):
  - Q Exactive Plus (MS:1002634) with an EASY-nLC.
  - Method `150min_Top10_proteome`, Full MS / dd-MS2 TopN.
  - HCD at NCE 27, Orbitrap MS2 at 17,500.
  - MS1 scan range 300-1750 m/z.
- **Instrument mismatch.** PRIDE's structured instrument "Q Exactive" is correct only for the excluded zar runs, whose headers say Q Exactive + nanoAcquity.
- **Fractionation:** SDS-PAGE (PRIDE:0000568), 6 slices, giving `comment[fraction identifier]` 1-6.
- **Digestion:** trypsin (MS:1001251). The search used the Trypsin/P rule (rule 2).
- **Tolerances** (mqpar group 1): precursor 4.5 ppm (main search; the first search was 20 ppm) and fragment 20 ppm (FTMS).
- **Modifications** (mqpar group 1; there are no fixed modifications):
  - Variable: Oxidation (M), Acetyl (Protein N-term), Phospho (STY), Carbamidomethyl (C) and `mmts`.
  - `mmts` = Methylthio (C), UNIMOD:39. It is +45.988 Da on C in `modificationSpecificPeptides.txt`.
  - SILAC labels: Arg10 = UNIMOD:267 and Lys8 = UNIMOD:259, written `MT=Variable` (rule 11).
  - The paper and PRIDE text call carbamidomethyl *fixed*. The deposited search had it variable, and the SDRF records the search as run (rule 5).

## Excluded runs (out of scope; BLOCKED.md section added)
The excluded files are:
- `710072209zar_k562_A.raw`, `710072210zar_k562_B.raw`
- `710072211zar_k562IM_A.raw`, `710072212zar_k562IM_B.raw`

- `710072215zar_HS5IM_A.raw`, `710072216zar_HS5IM_B.raw`

Why they are excluded:
- They are single-shot in-solution digests of K-562 and HS-5, with and without imatinib, run on a Q Exactive with a nanoAcquity.
- `samplenames.xls` says the +IM runs are "not discussed in this manuscript". The paper does not describe the others either.
- They were searched at multiplicity 2 (parameter group 0, LFQ on), but no source documents any SILAC labelling of them.
- Light is 98.1-99.3% of L+H intensity in all eight, including HS-5, so no heavy-labelled material is evident.
- A heavy channel with no documented content cannot be annotated (rule 7).

The other deposited files are not data runs: `MaxQuantOutput.tar.gz` and `andromeda.rar` are search output, and `samplenames.xls` is the run key.
