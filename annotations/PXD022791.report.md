**⚠️ DOUBLE-COUNTING WARNING — for any consumer combining these two SDRFs.**
`annotations/PXD019515.sdrf.tsv` and `annotations/PXD022791.sdrf.tsv` describe **the same 12 raw
files** in 12 of their rows. Concatenating them yields **36 rows describing 24 distinct runs**, and
would double-count **all 9 single cells** (3 HeLa, 3 motor neurons, 3 interneurons) and all 3 blanks.
Every biological conclusion in the paper — the MN-vs-IN differential analysis at n=3 per group —
would silently become n=6 per group against duplicated spectra. **Consumers must pick exactly one of
the two accessions.** PXD022791 (this file) is the better single choice: it is a strict superset and
loses nothing. PXD019515 is the better choice if you want *only* the cited, biological runs.

**These two SDRFs are byte-identical on the 12 shared rows.** I diffed all 43 columns × 12 shared
rows programmatically: **zero differing cells, including `assay name`** (I deliberately kept
PXD019515's row order and `run 1`–`run 12` numbering so the overlap is diffable with `diff`). The
optimisation runs are appended as `run 13`–`run 24`. I did not modify any PXD019515 file.

### `cell-lines` was again NOT declared — decided independently, same answer, *stronger* tension

I re-derived the constraint from the resolved template rather than trusting the prior report.
`spec/scripts/resolve_templates.py` on `cell-lines@1.1.0` gives:

```
characteristics[cell line] | requirement=required | allow_not_available=False | allow_not_applicable=False
```

Both escape hatches are shut. So declaring `cell-lines` obliges a real cell-line value on **every**
row. PXD022791 contains **9 rows that have no cell line**: the 6 LCM-excised primary neurons and the
3 cell-free blanks. No legal value exists for them. `cell-lines` is therefore undeclarable, exactly as
in PXD019515 — the blocking rows are literally the same nine files.

**But the cost is materially higher here, and I want that on the record.** In PXD019515 only 3 of 12
rows (25%) were cell-line material. In PXD022791 it is **15 of 24 (63%)** — 3 single HeLa cells plus
12 HeLa digest-standard runs. So this deposition is majority-HeLa, and the spec still forbids naming
the cell line, because a single-template SDRF must satisfy the strictest row. I considered and
rejected splitting the deposition into two SDRFs (a `cell-lines` one for the 15 HeLa rows, a plain one
for the 9 others): the brief asks for one artifact per deposition, and a split would fracture the
Fig. 2A→2B→4 workflow narrative that is the entire point of the paper. Recorded as the same spec
limitation already logged in `annotations/BLOCKED.md`.

HeLa identity is preserved the same way as in PXD019515 — via `characteristics[cell type]` =
`NT=HeLa cell;AC=CLO:0003684`, CLO being explicitly allowed by that column's resolved validator
(`ontologies: [cl, bto, clo]`). Consistency with PXD019515 is mandatory here, not merely nice: the
same three HeLa raw files appear in both.

### The 0.5 ng HeLa digest runs are NOT single cells — this is the central classification

The paper is unambiguous: *"MS acquisition and FAIMS settings were evaluated using **0.5 ng aliquots
of commercial HeLa protein digest standard**, an amount **equivalent to 2–3 cells**."* The reagent is
"Pierce HeLa protein digest standard ... purchased from Thermo Fisher" (Material section).

The trap here is the phrase "equivalent to 2–3 cells". That is a **mass equivalence**, not a cell
count. Writing `cells per well = 2` or `3` would fabricate cells that never existed: this is a
purchased, pre-digested peptide standard that never saw a nanowell and was never a cell in this
experiment. Accordingly these 12 rows carry:

- `characteristics[sample type]` = `NT=standard;AC=PRIDE:0000908` — verified via `getChildren` to be
  one of the **5 direct children** of PRIDE:0000895, so it satisfies the validator's `parent_term`
  cleanly (unlike `negative control`, below).
- `characteristics[cells per well]` = `not applicable` — **not** `0`, and **not** `2`/`3`. The blanks
  get `0` because a blank nanowell genuinely contained zero cells; the digest standard has no well and
  no cell-count concept at all. `allow_not_applicable=true` (verified).
- `characteristics[single cell isolation protocol]` = `not applicable` — required, but
  `allow_not_applicable=true`. Nothing was isolated; the vial was pipetted.
- `characteristics[cell identifier]` = `not applicable` — same.
- `characteristics[biological replicate]` = `1` on all 12. **Rep1/2/3 in the filenames are technical,
  not biological**: the paper says *"Error bars indicate standard deviations based on 3 **replicate
  measurements**"* of one commercial lot. One source material → one biological replicate. They share
  `source name` = `HeLa_digest_standard` and carry `comment[technical replicate]` = 1/2/3 per arm.

They retain HeLa provenance (`organism`, `organism part`, `cell type`, `disease`, and the Cellosaurus
donor demographics), because a HeLa digest *is* HeLa-derived material — `material type` = `cell line`.

## Conflicts found and how they were resolved

**1. PRIDE `modifications` vs the paper — PRIDE is incomplete.** PRIDE lists only `iodoacetamide
derivatized residue`. The paper's Data-analysis section lists **three**: N-terminal protein
acetylation and methionine oxidation as variable, carbamidomethylation of cysteine as fixed. The paper
wins per the brief. Annotated `UNIMOD:4` (TA=C, Fixed), `UNIMOD:35` (TA=M, Variable), `UNIMOD:1`
(`PP=Protein N-term`, Variable). **UNIMOD:1 = Acetyl and UNIMOD:21 = Phospho were both verified live
against OLS** and are not swapped; there is no phospho in this study. The N-terminal position is in
`PP=`, never `TA=`.

**2. `OTOT` filenames still carry the `300ITMS2` token — a naming artifact, flagged.** The suffix
`300ITMS2` decodes as the ion trap's 300 ms MS2 maximum injection time (*"3 × 10⁴/300 ms when the ion
trap was used for detection"*). It is therefore **self-contradictory on the three `OTOT` files**,
where MS2 was the orbitrap at *"1 × 10⁵/500 ms"*. I judged `OTOT` authoritative (it is the token that
encodes Fig. 2A's actual variable) and `300ITMS2` a stale filename template carried across all 24
files. Listed as an open item — this is the one place I overrode a filename token.

**3. PRIDE `instruments` vs the paper — no conflict.** PRIDE says `Orbitrap Eclipse`; the paper says
"Orbitrap Eclipse Tribrid". `MS:1003029` "Orbitrap Eclipse" is the only MS term and covers both.
Raw headers were **not** range-fetched: with nothing to adjudicate, there was no question to answer.

**4. PRIDE says neurons were "manually selected"; the paper says "selected".** Immaterial — both agree
on LCM excision via the PALM MicroBeam.

## The FAIMS CV axis is NOT expressible — a real, unavoidable information loss

**The `2CV` vs `3CV` arms are the study's own Fig. 2A comparison, and this SDRF cannot encode it.**
Rows `FAIMS_2CV_HELA_Point5ng_OTIT_HCD_*` and `FAIMS_3CV_HELA_Point5ng_OTIT_HCD_*` are **identical in
all 43 columns except `assay name` and `comment[data file]`** — verified programmatically.

I checked this properly before conceding it. Searching all 223 TERMS.tsv rows for FAIMS / compensation
voltage / ion mobility yields exactly two candidates, `comment[ms min im]` and `comment[ms max im]`
(PRIDE:0000841/842, licensed by `ms-proteomics`). **Both are unusable here, on two independent
grounds:**

- **Semantics**: their `values` field is `numeric (1/K0 or Vs/cm2)` — TIMS-style reduced mobility, not
  a FAIMS compensation voltage in volts. Different physical quantity.
- **Syntax**: the resolved validator pattern is `^[\d.]+$`, which **has no minus sign**. The CVs are
  **−55 V, −70 V, −85 V**. They are literally unrepresentable.

Abusing those columns to smuggle in `55`/`85` would be a fabrication that validates cleanly — exactly
the silent-corruption class. I left them out. **The 2CV/3CV distinction is recoverable only from
`comment[data file]`.** This is a spec gap, not an annotation defect, and it deserves an upstream
issue.

## Key decisions and evidence

**Cleavage agent differs between the two arms — deliberately.** The 12 biological rows carry
`Trypsin` (MS:1001251) **+ `Lys-C`** (MS:1001309), inherited from PXD019515 (both reagents are in the
paper's Material list and the cited nanoPOTS one-pot protocol uses both). The **12 digest rows carry
`Trypsin` only**, second column `not applicable`. Rationale: Lys-C belongs to the nanoPOTS workflow
applied to *cells*; the Pierce HeLa standard arrives pre-digested and is a tryptic digest, consistent
with the tryptic (plus semi-tryptic second-stage) search specificity. Note `cleavage agent details` is
`required` with `allow_not_available=false` — so `not available` was **not** a legal escape, and the
column forced an assertion. Trypsin is the best-evidenced one, but **this is an inference from product
identity, not a stated fact**, and it is the weakest technical claim in the file. Flagged.

**HeLa donor demographics are Cellosaurus-sourced, re-verified live for this file.** I queried
`api.cellosaurus.org/cell-line/CVCL_0030` directly rather than copying PXD019515: `AG 30Y6M`,
`SX Female`, `CC Population: African American` → HANCESTRO:0568, `CC Derived from site: In situ;
Uterus, cervix; UBERON=UBERON_0000002`, `OX NCBI_TaxID=9606`. These columns are licensed by `human`,
not `cell-lines`, so dropping `cell-lines` does not un-license them.

**Disease** → `cervical adenocarcinoma` (MONDO:0005153). Cellosaurus asserts the finer
`DI NCIt; C27677; Human papillomavirus-related endocervical adenocarcinoma`, and the resolved
validator does allow `ncit` — but TERMS.tsv lists `MONDO, EFO, DOID, PATO` in preference order, so
MONDO wins and the HPV sub-claim is dropped rather than smuggled in.

**The HeLa OLS trap was hit, in both ontologies.** `mode="fuzzy"` on `HeLa` in **CLO** returns
`HeLa-MAGI-CCR5`, `HeLa-R5-16`, `HELA-S3`, `HeLa derived cell line cell`, `HeLa NR1` — **the parental
term is not in the top 5 at all**. Only `searchClasses(query="HeLa cell", mode="exact")` returns
`CLO:0003684`. In **EFO**, fuzzy returns `EFO:0001185` (HeLa) first but with `HEp-2` (EFO:0006438) in
the same list. `CVCL_0030` → `CLO:0003684` and `EFO:0001185` were both confirmed against the live
Cellosaurus `DR` cross-references, which is the check that actually closes this.

**`negative control` (PRIDE:0000905) on blanks — PXD019515's open item #5 is now resolved, in its
favour.** That report worried the term is not a *direct* child of PRIDE:0000895. It is not:
`getChildren(PRIDE:0000895)` returns exactly 5 (QC sample, single cell, multiplex sample, standard,
environmental sample), and `negative control` sits one level below, under `Quality Control Sample`
(PRIDE:0000544). But the column's own validator **lists "negative control" among its `examples`**,
alongside `bulk control`, `buffer control`, `plate control`, `calibrator` — *all* of which are
grandchildren via PRIDE:0000544. The validator plainly means descendant, not direct child. The same
holds for `beam-type CID` (MS:1000422), which is likewise a grandchild of MS:1000044. Kept as-is; the
concern was unfounded.

**Factor.** `factor value[cell type]`, mirroring `characteristics[cell type]` — identical to
PXD019515 (mandatory, since 12 rows are shared). The optimisation arm's true factor is
fragmentation × MS2 analyzer × CV count, which **no single factor column can express** (and the CV
component is unrepresentable at all — see above). The digest rows therefore carry `HeLa cell` as the
factor value, which is *true* but is not the variable Fig. 2A tests. Flagged as an open item.

**Donor structure.** All 6 neurons come from **one** spinal cord donor (`spinal_donor_1`; the paper
says "human spinal tissue", singular). The headline MN-vs-IN comparison is n=1 donor, n=3 cells per
group. All 15 HeLa-derived rows share `HeLa_donor`. Both facts are visible in
`characteristics[individual]` rather than buried.

**Anatomy left at `spinal cord` (UBERON:0002240) for all 6 neurons** — inherited unchanged from
PXD019515, whose reasoning I re-checked and agree with: `ventral horn` (UBERON:0002257) exists but no
UBERON term exists for the spinal intermediate zone, so annotating MNs more specifically than INs
would falsely imply anatomy was the annotation axis. The MN/IN distinction is carried by
`characteristics[cell type]` (CL:0011001 vs CL:0005000), which is the paper's actual claim.
