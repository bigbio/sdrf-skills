## Study
"Quantitative analysis of SUMO1 and SUMO2 proteomes in human lung A549 cells during influenza A virus infection or heat-shock". The paper is Domingues P et al., Global Reprogramming of Host SUMOylation during Influenza Virus Infection. *Cell Rep* 2015;13(7):1467-80, doi:10.1016/j.celrep.2015.10.001, PMID 26549460, PMC4660286. The Supplemental Experimental Procedures (mmc1.pdf) were read from PMC OA S3.

The study ran three triple-SILAC experiments in A549 lines that stably express the TAP tag only, TAP-SUMO1 or TAP-SUMO2. In each experiment the lysates were mixed 1:1:1. About 1% was kept as the **crude** sample and about 99% was **TAP-purified**. Both were run on SDS-PAGE and cut into 13 gel slices, which were measured on a Q Exactive (HCD, DDA). The data were searched with MaxQuant 1.3.0.5 against human plus IAV A/WSN/1933.

## Open items / limitations
- `comment[precursor mass tolerance]` is `not available`. MaxQuant 1.3 `parameters.txt`/`summary.txt` and the Supplemental parameter table do not give it, and no mqpar.xml was deposited. As a result `convert-openms` exits 1.
- Reduction and alkylation reagents are `not available`. The paper cites "Shevchenko et al., 2006" in-gel digestion without naming the reagents.
- Passage number and sample storage temperature are `not available`.
- `characteristics[treatment]` "heat shock" has no NCIT/EFO term (validator warning). "untreated" also warns, as it does throughout the campaign.
- **Observation (HS_SUMO2):** trace influenza NP (17 peptides) and NS1 (4 peptides) show up in the heat-shock search with the IAV channel pattern. The level is about 400-fold lower than in the IAV experiments (about 0.1‰ of channel intensity in crude, versus about 47‰), and M1, HA and PB2 are absent. This looks like contamination or LC carryover, not infection. The protocol states the heat-shock experiment had no infection, so the HS rows are "uninfected".
- The Supplemental methods describe two MaxQuant analyses per IAV experiment ("global" and "slice-by-slice"), but only the global txt was deposited. Both use the same parameters.
- The search FASTA uses GenBank IAV accessions, whereas PRIDE says "UniProtKB reference proteomes". This is noted only; no SDRF column depends on it.
