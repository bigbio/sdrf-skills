## Key decisions and evidence

**Instrument assigned per file from the raw headers, not from the paper.** All 124 `.raw` files were
HTTP-range-probed (first 64 KB, decoded utf-16-le): **88 Orbitrap Eclipse, 36 Orbitrap Exploris 480**,
zero unresolved. This independently reproduces the paper's split ("An Orbitrap Eclipse ... was used to
compare reagent compositions"; "An Orbitrap Exploris 480 ... to compare one-step and multistep ... and
different nanowell substrates") and the FragPipe tables (Reagents = 88 runs; the two Exploris analyses
share 12 runs, 24+24−12 = 36). Headers also expose the acquisition directories
(`D:\Madi\2023\01\Reagent_Selection` vs `D:\data\Madi\MS\2022\October\Substrates`), which corroborate
the same partition. PRIDE lists both instruments but assigns neither to a file; every one of the 90
annotated rows carries the model read out of its own file.

**Modifications: all three variable, including Carbamidomethyl.** Paper: *"The **variable**
modifications included N-terminal acetylation (42.0106 u), methionine oxidation (15.9949 u), and
cysteine carboxyamidomethylation (57.02146 u) to account for alkylation and potential
post-translational modifications."* Carbamidomethyl is **not** fixed here — deliberately, because only
the multistep arm is alkylated (IAA), so a fixed C+57 would be wrong for the 80 one-step cells. The
single search covers both arms by making it variable. `MT=Fixed` would have validated cleanly and been
false. PRIDE's `modifications` field says *"No PTMs are included in the dataset"* — contradicted by the
Methods; the peer-reviewed Methods were followed.

**Cleavage agents: Trypsin + Lys-C on all 90 rows.** Both arms use Promega Rapid Digestion
Trypsin/Lys-C (one-step) or sequential Lys-C→trypsin (multistep). The *search* specified "strict
trypsin", but this column describes digestion, not the search — matching the `PXD052416` precedent.

**Prep arm reconstructed from filenames + Methods, and it reconciles with the paper's own n.** The
substrate arms are exactly **10 / 10 / 10**, matching *"Ten single cells were prepared using each of

**The one-step reagent used downstream is Protease + DDM**, stated twice (paper: *"The one-step mixture

## Honest gaps — read these before trusting the file

**1. The 12 QC runs are excluded because their material is unidentified — not because they lack a
sample.** This is a weaker justification than the blank-well exclusion and is the annotation's softest
point. The paper's Methods never mention the QC injections at all; PRIDE never mentions them; the
Xcalibur sequence fields in the raw headers (`Study`, `Client`, `Laboratory`, `Company`) are **empty
placeholders**, and the only hint is a method filename (`..._2x5nguL_Stg_070QC.meth`) giving load, not
provenance. So there is no evidence of **what** the 10 ng digest is — in particular no evidence that it
is HeLa at all, let alone whether it is an in-house bulk digest of their own ATCC HeLa (→ parental
`CVCL_0030`) or a commercial Pierce standard (→ **HeLa S3**, `CVCL_0058`, a *different* Cellosaurus
entry, per the brief's rule). Those two answers are mutually exclusive and both plausible for this lab.
Since `characteristics[cell line]` forbids both reserved words, there is no way to annotate these rows
without picking one — i.e. fabricating. They are excluded and recorded in `BLOCKED.md`.
*This is a third exclusion shape*, distinct from the two already in `BLOCKED.md`: not a sample-less run
and not a missing channel map, but **a real sample whose identity is undetermined**.

**2. `comment[precursor mass tolerance]` = `20 ppm` is an inference, not a quotation.** The paper says
*"a precursor mass tolerance of 20 **u**"*. Taken literally that is 20 Da — an open search — which
contradicts "strict trypsin" plus 1% FDR, and the same Methods use ppm correctly elsewhere ("m/z
tolerance of 10 ppm") and `u` correctly for masses ("mass range of 500–5,000 u"). 20 ppm is the
MSFragger default for Orbitrap data and is almost certainly what was run. The column's pattern
(`number + ppm/Da`) cannot express `20 u` anyway. Flagged rather than silently normalized — a reviewer
may prefer `not available`.

**3. The DDM axis of the reagent design is not machine-readable in SDRF.** The four one-step arms are a
2×2 (DDM ± × DTT ±). The DTT axis maps to `comment[reduction reagent]`; **there is no licensed column
for a lysis surfactant**, so Protease-only vs Protease+DDM would be indistinguishable in every spec
column. To avoid losing the study's headline variable, the full arm is carried in
`comment[sample preparation batch]` (free text; its own description is "plate, chip, **processing
batch**", and each reagent arm is exactly a processing batch) and mirrored into
`factor value[sample preparation batch]`. This is the most contestable modeling choice in the file;
`characteristics[treatment]` was rejected because its `values` are NCIT/EFO and a lysis reagent is not
a biological perturbation of the cell.

**4. Two DDM+DTT well groups were merged.** On chips 1–2 the one-step arms occupy well columns 4–11
(Protease 4/5, DDM 6/7, DTT 8/9, DDM+DTT 10/11), but six extra wells at **column 1** are also named
`DDMDTT` (`1_DDMDTT_1A1`, `2_DDMDTT_2B1`, …). No source documents the plate layout. They are annotated
by their literal filename reagent composition (Protease+DTT+DDM). The alternative reading — that
column 1 is the multistep control, whose lysis buffer is also DDM+DTT — is *disfavored* because the
paper places the one-step-vs-multistep comparison on the **Exploris**, and these are Eclipse files. Both
readings agree on the reagent; they differ on whether these 6 cells are one-step. Flagged as
defensible, not certain.

**5. `factor value[...]` is technical, not biological.** All 90 cells are the same HeLa population; the
study varies only sample prep. Factors are `sample preparation batch` (prep arm) and
`nanopots chip version` (glass vs polypropylene) — the two axes the paper actually compares.
