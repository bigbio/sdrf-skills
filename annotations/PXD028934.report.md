## Sample design and channel maps (all stated in the paper's Methods, Results and Fig. 7 legend)

### Exp 1 supernatant
- **Pulse protocol:** cells were grown ≥7 generations in light Lys0/Arg0, then pulsed in heavy D4 Lys-4 + 13C6 Arg-6 for 15 min at 25 °C, then heat shocked for 20 min at 45 °C.
- **Channels:**
  - light = pre-existing proteome
  - heavy = proteins newly synthesised during the pulse
- **Data check:** median H/L is 0.089–0.102 per replicate, which fits a short pulse. The paper reports a median H/L of about 11% in the TCL.
- **Replicates:** Results say Exp 1 used "four biological replicates". The search has four MaxQuant experiments, MSZ20_S_1–4, one file each. Each has about 1,200 protein groups, and pairwise intensity r is 0.92–0.94. These are replicates, not high-pH fractions. Sup_k → biological replicate k, and both channels of a file share k because they come from the same culture.

### LiP
- **Pulsed culture:** grown light, then pulsed 15 min in heavy "Lys-8 / Arg-10". It was **not heat shocked**. The lysate got 0 to 20 ng proteinase K per µg for 5 min at 20 °C, then each PK-treated lysate was mixed 1:1 with a reference.
- **Reference:** a separately grown medium-labelled culture (Lys4/Arg6) with no PK.
- **Channels:**
  - light = pre-existing proteome (PK-treated)
  - heavy = newly synthesised proteome (PK-treated)
  - medium = untreated reference
- **Data check (peptides.txt):**
  - In Ref, the M/L median is 0.98 (the 1:1 mix) and the summed H/L is 0.09 (a small pulse).
  - The summed L/M falls only at the highest PK amounts: 0.49 at "50" and 0.84 at "200", against 0.96–1.02 elsewhere. That fits PK digesting the light/heavy lysate while the medium reference stays intact.

## Conflicts found
1. **LiP heavy label naming.** The LiP paragraph says "D4 Lys-8 and 13C6 Arg-10". This contradicts itself: D4-Lys is +4 and 13C6-Arg is +6, the same as the medium channel. **Resolved as Lys8 = 13C6 15N2 (UNIMOD:259) and Arg10 = 13C6 15N4 (UNIMOD:267)**, for three reasons: the nominal masses, the 3-plex design that needs heavy ≠ medium, and the Exp 3 paragraph of the same Methods, which spells out 13C6 15N2 Lys-8 / 13C6 15N4 Arg-10.
2. **PK amounts.** PRIDE lists six amounts (no 0.01 ng/µg); the paper lists seven, and seven LiP files exist. The paper wins.
3. **Raw file format.** The deposit has bare `.baf` files rather than `.d` folders. They are recorded as deposited, because they are the Bruker vendor raw.

## Judgement calls for the reviewer to attack
1. **Encoding the LiP PK amount through `clinical-metadata`.** This uses `compound` = Proteinase K, `dose` in **mg/kg** (a w/w PK:lysate ratio, 1 ng/µg = 1000 mg/kg) and `treatment` = "proteinase K treatment". The PK is applied to a lysate, not to cells. The alternatives would be dropping the dose, which loses the experimental variable, or using `spiked compound`, which is semantically wrong.
2. **The channel and source model.** Light (pre-existing) and heavy (newly synthesised) from one lysate are separate sources that share the culture's biological replicate number. The single medium reference extract appears as 7 per-run "aliquot" sources with biological replicate 2 in the "untreated" group. The LiP heavy label is set to Lys8/Arg10 (UNIMOD:259/267) despite the paper's "D4"/"13C6" prefixes.
3. **The inferred values.**
   - Precursor 0.07 Da and fragment 40 ppm, taken from Beck 2015's MaxQuant QTOF settings, not from this deposit's parameters.
   - Sup_1–4 as biological replicates 1–4.
   - Beam-type CID (MS:1000422) for QTOF collision-cell CID, rather than generic CID (MS:1000133).
