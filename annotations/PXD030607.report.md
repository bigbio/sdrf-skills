scale across cell sizes, from giant (~250 µm) *Xenopus laevis* blastomeres to small (~35 µm) cultured
mouse hippocampal neurons, and is compared against nanoLC-MS.

## ⚠ PXD030607 is a byte-identical subset of PXD031955

**This is the headline finding and it must not be lost.** The two accessions are not merely related —
all 15 RAW files in PXD030607 are present in PXD031955 with **identical SHA-1 checksums and identical
byte sizes, 15/15**. Verified via `files/all` on both accessions, not by filename alone.

| | PXD030607 | PXD031955 |
|---|---|---|
| Submitted | 2021-12-22 | 2022-02-28 |
| Published | 2023-10-25 | — |
| RAW files | 15 | 18 |
| Total files | 37 | 46 |
| Cited by PMID:35464213 | **no** | **yes** ("…project accession number PXD031955") |

PXD031955 is a **strict superset**. The three RAW files it adds are exactly the ones this deposition
lacks, and they are the scientifically distinctive ones — the *actual single mouse neurons*:

```
2017-11-10_SC02_Single_Neuron_microtube_injection_5_min.RAW
2017-11-17_SC01._Single_Neuron_microvial_injection_5_min.RAW
2017-11-17_SC04_ziptip_Single_Neuron_microvial_injection_5_min.RAW
```

The paper's Data Repository section names **only PXD031955**. PXD030607 is the earlier, superseded
deposition; PXD031955 appears to be the resubmission the authors actually cite.

**Consequences the reviewer and any downstream consumer must weigh:**

1. Annotating both accessions produces **15 duplicated rows** describing the same raw bytes. If both
   SDRFs are contributed, any corpus-level reuse (quantms reprocessing, SDRF Explorer) will
   double-count these runs. A deduplication decision is needed **above** the level of this artifact —
   I cannot make it from inside PXD030607, and I did not touch PXD031955's files as instructed.
2. My reading is that **PXD031955 should be the annotated accession and PXD030607 should be dropped**,
   because it is a strict subset *and* the publication cites the superset. I am flagging this rather
   than acting on it.
3. The abstract's claim "Capillary microsampling enabled the isolation and transfer of individual
   neurons from the culture, identifying 37 proteins between three different cells" is **not
   supported by any file in PXD030607** — those three cells are the 2017-11-* runs, present only in
   PXD031955. A reader who takes PXD030607 as the paper's dataset cannot reproduce that claim.

## Key decisions and evidence

**Instrument — PRIDE is wrong twice, and the raw headers settle it.** PRIDE's `instruments` field lists
`Q Exactive` (generic) and `Orbitrap Fusion Lumos`. There is no generic Q Exactive here. The paper says
`Q Exactive Plus` for all CE-ESI-MS. I HTTP-range-read the first 256 KB of five deposited RAW files
across every arm and decoded utf-16-le: all four CE-MS files return **`Q Exactive Plus`**
(`MS:1002634`), and `2021-04-13_LP_AWCsinglecellV12_1.raw` returns **`Orbitrap Fusion Lumos`**
(`MS:1002732`). Paper and raw headers agree against PRIDE's submitter-entered field.

**Modifications — PRIDE contradicts the paper, and the paper is internally consistent.** PRIDE's
`data_processing_protocol` says "Static and dynamic modifications were **disabled for Xenopus data** and
enabled for analyzing mouse neuronal data." That is wrong for the Xenopus **LC-MS** subset. The paper's
Data Analysis section: "modifications of cysteine carbamidomethylation and methionine oxidation were
enabled for **neuroproteomics by CE-MS** and **Xenopus proteomics by LC-MS**."

This is not a coin-flip — the paper's sample prep independently forces the same conclusion:

- Xenopus **CE-MS**: "traditional bottom-up proteomics steps such as reduction, alkylation, and
  desalting were **eliminated** to reduce material losses" → no alkylation → carbamidomethyl is
  chemically impossible → mods `not applicable` (6 rows). This is a real, deliberate design feature of
  the low-input CE workflow, not missing metadata.
- Xenopus **LC-MS**: "the proteome was similarly processed, **including reduction and alkylation**, and
  desalted on a ZipTip" → Carbamidomethyl + Oxidation (3 rows).
- Mouse: explicit DTT reduction + iodoacetamide alkylation → Carbamidomethyl + Oxidation (6 rows).

So mods are enabled on 9 rows and `not applicable` on 6. PRIDE's blanket "disabled for Xenopus" would
have wrongly stripped them from the LC-MS rows. `UNIMOD:4` = Carbamidomethyl, `UNIMOD:35` = Oxidation —
neither is the UNIMOD:1/UNIMOD:21 pair, so the classic swap is not in play here.

**`single cell isolation protocol` = `not applicable` on all 15 rows — the enum has no member for this,
and that is the honest answer.** The enum is `fixed: FACS, cellenONE, microfluidics, laser capture
microdissection, manual picking, nanoPOTS, droplet microfluidics, acoustic droplet ejection`.

- Xenopus: **no cell was ever isolated.** The blastomere stays in the intact embryo; a pulled glass
  capillary aspirates its *contents* in situ. Nothing is picked, sorted, cut, or transferred. `manual
  picking` would be the tempting fit and it is wrong — it asserts a physical operation that did not
  happen. `not available` would also be wrong: it means "information exists but was not provided", and
  the information *was* provided in full. The property genuinely does not apply.
  `allow_not_applicable=true` permits this. **`annotations/PXD006905.sdrf.tsv` — Xenopus blastomere
  capillary microsampling from the same lab — made exactly this call**, so this is consistent with repo
  precedent rather than a novel reading.
- Mouse: bulk culture lysate, no single-cell isolation at all.

**The Xenopus samples are PARTIAL cells, and `cells per well = 1` is the weakest cell in this
artifact.** The abstract is explicit: 18 ng is "**~0.2% of the total cellular proteome**" — the aspirate
is a subcellular fraction of one blastomere, and the paper calls this "subcellular analysis". TERMS.tsv
says "use 1 for true single cells". I set `1` because the field counts *contributing cells*, which is
exactly one identified cell, and there is no enum or column expressing "a fraction of one cell". But a
consumer reading `cells per well = 1` + `sample type = single cell` will reasonably infer a **whole**
cell was measured, which is false. I am flagging this as a genuine spec gap for the reviewer rather
than papering over it. Same reasoning for `sample type = single cell` (`PRIDE:0000897`): the sample
derives from one identified cell, and PRIDE has no "subcellular aliquot" child.

**Mouse rows are NOT single cells and are typed as such.** Paper: "to process the entire cell
population, the neurons were rinsed … pelleted … lysed". These are dilutions of a bulk digest that
*estimate to* single-neuron amounts — a sensitivity surrogate, not a single cell. Typed
`sample type = bulk control` (`PRIDE:0000907`), `pooled sample = pooled`, `cells per well = not
applicable`, `cell identifier = not applicable`. Reading the filenames as single neurons would have
been an easy and serious error, especially since the *real* single-neuron runs exist — in PXD031955.

**Blastomere identity lives in `cell identifier`, because no ontology has these terms.** I checked:
`searchClasses(query="V11 blastomere", ontologyId="cl")` → `numFound: 0`. CL's nearest term is
`blastoderm cell` (`CL:0000353`, synonym *blastomere*), which is correct but cannot express V11 vs V12
vs D11. `characteristics[cell type]` is restricted by TERMS.tsv to CL/BTO/CLO, so the named identities
cannot go there. They go in `characteristics[cell identifier]` (`free text pattern`), which is exactly
its purpose: `Xla_E6_V11`, `Xla_E6_V12`, `Xla_E1_D11`, `Xla_V12_LC`. XAO (Xenopus Anatomy Ontology)
*does* carry blastomere terms, but XAO is not an allowed ontology for any column here — note that
`PXD006905` put `XAO:1000015` in `characteristics[developmental stage]`, whose TERMS.tsv `values` field
allows **EFO only**. I did not copy that defect; I used `cleavage stage` (`UBERON:0000107`), which is
reachable within EFO and validates clean.

**`hippocampus` is an OLS trap and the brief called it.** Smart-mode
`searchClasses(query="hippocampus", ontologyId="uberon")` returns a single confident hit:
**`CA1 field of hippocampus` (`UBERON:0003881`)** — wrong, and it would have silently narrowed the whole
mouse arm to one subfield. Fuzzy mode exposes 56 candidates. The paper says only "the mouse
hippocampus" / "hippocampal neurons", which is the standard whole-hippocampus dissection, so I used
`hippocampal formation` (`UBERON:0002421`) — the broader, correct unit — rather than `Ammon's horn`
(`UBERON:0001954`) or any subfield the evidence does not support.

**Acquisition parameters are per-arm, not global**, and were read individually from Methods:

| | Xenopus CE-MS | Xenopus LC-MS | mouse CE-MS |
|---|---|---|---|
| instrument | Q Exactive Plus | Orbitrap Fusion Lumos | Q Exactive Plus |
| collision energy | 28 NCE | 30 NCE | 32 NCE |
| fragment tolerance | 0.02 Da | 0.6 Da | 0.02 Da |
| MS2 analyzer | orbitrap `MS:1000484` | radial ejection linear ion trap `MS:1000083` | orbitrap |
| precursor m/z range | 350–900 | not stated | 350–1800 |

The 0.6 Da fragment tolerance is not an outlier to be normalized away: Methods state "fragment mass
tolerance, 0.02 Da (Q Exactive Plus) and **0.6 Da (Lumos)**", and independently "The precursor ions were
fragmented in the **ion trap**" on the Lumos. Ion-trap MS2 → 0.6 Da. The two statements corroborate each
other, so the LC-MS rows get 0.6 Da + ion trap. Precursor tolerance is 10 ppm on all 15 rows.

## Design findings (recorded, not defects)

**There are no biological replicates in this deposition.** Every source is n=1: one V11 cell, one V12
cell, one LC-MS V12 cell, one D11 cell, one bulk mouse digest. All 13 multi-run groups are **technical**
replication (repeat injections). `characteristics[biological replicate] = 1` on all 15 rows is therefore
correct and is not a filler value — it faithfully records that this is a platform demonstration, not a
powered comparison. No biological conclusion about V11 vs V12 vs D11 is supported by these data.

**The factor is the weakest part of the design encoding.** I used `factor value[cell type]` (blastoderm
cell, 9 rows / hippocampal neuron, 6 rows), which tracks the paper's actual thesis — scalability "across
broad cellular dimensions", ~250 µm Xenopus cells vs ~35 µm mouse neurons. But this deposition has **no
biological factor at all**; it is a methods benchmark whose real variables are platform (CE vs nanoLC),
load (400 ng / 18 ng / 5 ng / 500–125 pg), and cell identity. Those are carried by `comment[instrument]`,
`source name`, and `characteristics[cell identifier]` respectively, not by a factor column. A reviewer
may reasonably prefer a different factor; I do not think a strictly correct one exists here.

**`characteristics[cell diameter]` deliberately omitted.** The abstract's ~250 µm / ~35 µm are
model-level generalizations, and V11, V12 and D11 are demonstrably *different* sizes (that is precisely
why D11 yields 5 ng against V11/V12's 18 ng). Stamping 250 µm on all three would fabricate a
measurement the paper never made per cell.
